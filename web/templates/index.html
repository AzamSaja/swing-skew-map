System Architecture & The 7 Decisions Flow

Interactive visualization of Bert Trading's 7 architectural decisions and 5 trap guardrails.

The 7 Decisions Pipeline

Decision #1 Delta Solver
Probability-Equidistant Strikes

Finds 25-delta Call and 25-delta Put using Black-Scholes. Prevents percentage moneyness distortion between calm vs volatile stocks.

Decision #2 Expiry Rule
Target Monthly Contract

Selects standard 3rd Friday contract (~30-45 DTE). Avoids front-week noise and gamma decay distortion across names.

Decision #3 Normalization
Skew vs. Vol Points Dual Core

Normalized Skew for intra-sector ranking; un-normalized Vol Points for cross-sector comparisons (Trap #2 guard).

Decision #4 Benchmark
Independent Sector Benchmarking

Benchmarks against broad neutral universes (SPY, QQQ, IWM, and ^JKSE for IDX), preventing personal watchlist bias from laundering back as market signals.

Decision #5 Quality Bar
Open Interest & Liquidity Check

Requires min open interest on target strikes. Thin, untrustworthy chains are tagged and displayed as hollow rings rather than quietly dropped.

Decision #6 Sanity Ceiling
Trap #4 Quote Artifact Filter

Enforces hard ceilings (|Skew| ≤ 1.25). Rejects single-leg bad marks (like the Duke Energy -1.43 bug) before they distort sector metrics.

Decision #7 History Storage
Week-over-Week Delta Tracking

Stores daily snapshots in data/snapshots/. Level is structural; change is the true signal. Tracks Δ Skew and quadrant flips.

IDX Module Emerging Mkt
Realized Volatility Asymmetry

For markets without listed equity options (IDX), computes downside vs. upside semi-variance skew against IHSG (^JKSE).

The 5 Traps & Guardrails

Trap #1: Never Quote an Index Put/Call Ratio

86% of SPY put open interest sits in deep tail crash insurance (<0.10 delta). Counting the whole chain returns arbitrary sentiment. The system isolates 25-delta probability-equidistant strikes.

Trap #2: Don't Compare Sectors Using Normalized Skew

ATM IV varies 3x between Utilities and Semis. Dividing by a low ATM IV makes Utilities float to the top of fear rankings every day. Cross-sector charts use raw Vol Points.

Trap #3: Check Agreement Before Trusting Sector Reads

Averages hide internal splits. If constituent agreement is below 60%, the system automatically attaches a breadth caveat flag.

Trap #4: One Bad Quote Inverts an Entire Name

A single wide spread or bad mark can flip a stock to a false extreme bid and drag correlation down. Handled via a strict sanity ceiling (|Skew| ≤ 1.25).

Trap #5: A Dated Catalyst is Not Sentiment

If an earnings announcement lands inside the measured expiry, front options carry elevated IV due to the event. The engine flags this as Event Premium, preventing false fear readings.
